The Pay page contains two tabs: **Send** and **Receive**. Send is the default and can also be opened
with an asset, a network, both values, or a prefilled address in the route.

## Send flow

<Steps>
    <Step title="Choose the asset and network">
        The header selector carries an explicit stablecoin/network pair.
        Changing it replaces the route so refresh and navigation preserve the
        choice.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Enter the recipient">
        Paste or type a valid destination address. A scanned UniFi QR can
        prefill the same payment data.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Enter the amount">
        The form loads balance and estimated-fee information for the selected
        pair. Mobile layouts use the product's numeric keypad instead of
        depending on the browser keypad.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Review">
        Review the stablecoin, network, recipient, amount, memo when present,
        and fee treatment before authorizing.
    </Step>
    <Step title="Authorize and submit">
        Email accounts confirm with authenticator OTP. MetaMask accounts approve
        Permit2 when needed, then sign the payment-specific request before it is
        sent to UniFi.
    </Step>
</Steps>

## Fee mode

UniFi supports fee-exclusive and fee-inclusive amounts. Exclusive mode adds the fee to the entered
payment; inclusive mode accounts for the fee inside it. The review surface is the place to confirm
which mode applies.

## Connected-wallet approval

When the Permit2 allowance is insufficient, the UI shows an approval step before signing. The
approval is an onchain ERC-20 transaction; the later payment authorization is a short-lived
signature. User-rejected, disconnected-wallet, wrong-network, insufficient-gas, and unavailable-
network errors have distinct messages in the current implementation.

### Why connected wallets may need a network token

When you pay with MetaMask or another self-custodial wallet, Permit2 must first be authorized to use
the selected token. If the current allowance is insufficient, your wallet submits an onchain approval
transaction. The network charges gas for this transaction, so the wallet must hold a small amount of
the network's native token.

After the approval is confirmed, you sign the payment authorization and submit the payment. Later
payments can skip the approval step while the existing allowance remains sufficient.

Email-based custodial wallets and AWS KMS wallets use a managed authorization flow, so you do not
need to fund a separate native-token approval from a connected wallet.

## Result

Successful submission opens a result sheet with the new receipt ID. The receipt begins in a
processing/submitted state and can later advance to confirmed and finalized.
